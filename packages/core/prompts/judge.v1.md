You are judging two short business sketches for an owner who wants to build a software business. Decide which ONE of the two the owner should rather build.

Judge on these criteria, in this order of importance:
1. need: confidence that buyers really need it and will pay for it.
2. nonTrivial: it is not trivial. It needs engineering, data or industry expertise; a person with a spreadsheet, or one prompt to a chatbot with the data, cannot do it.
3. earn: it earns money for the buyer (more revenue) rather than only saving them money.
4. edge: a concrete edge against named incumbents. "None known" or a vague edge is weak.
5. evidence: the strength of the cited evidence. Each cited signal shows who wrote it (its voice: operator, customer, neutral, vendor or untagged), and each signal or library entry that was assessed shows a strength from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) with how direct the source is. First-hand and high-strength evidence counts most; vendor and untagged voices count least. A sketch with no cited evidence is weakest here.

- An earlier criterion outweighs the later ones; use the later ones to break near-ties.
- Position does not matter: Sketch A is not better for being shown first, nor Sketch B for being shown last.
