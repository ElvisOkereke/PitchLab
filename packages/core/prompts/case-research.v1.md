Use web search to answer these four questions. Search for primary sources first: industry surveys and benchmark reports, government statistics (e.g. census business counts, Statistics Canada, US Census County Business Patterns, BLS wage data), trade associations, vendor pricing pages and directories.

1. Buyer economics: what does the problem cost one buyer? Find the inputs for the cost, not a total: how often the failure happens, how many units it affects, what each occurrence costs (lost revenue per lead or job, staff hours and hourly wage, fees, churn). 2. Reachable buyers: how many businesses of the buyer type exist in {{market}}? Prefer a count from a census, association membership figure or a directory listing over a market-size dollar estimate. Say exactly what the count covers (which countries, which business type, which year).
3. Competitor pricing: for each existing product, service or manual workaround that addresses the problem, give its name, URL, what it actually checks or does, its limitation for this buyer, and its published price (say plainly when the price is not published).
4. Why now: what has changed recently (technology, platform changes, regulation, buyer behaviour, costs) that makes this problem bigger or the solution newly possible? Give dates.
