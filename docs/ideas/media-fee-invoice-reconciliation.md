# Media & data-fee invoice reconciliation, paid only from credits recovered

Match what DTC and e-commerce brands were billed for ads, audience data and direct/affiliate placements against what was delivered and what could convert, then file the credit and makegood claims for a share of what comes back.

## Problem

Brands accept that a lot of ad spend is wasted and can't say which part. Some of that loss is a billing problem, not a performance problem. Third-party audience data adds a premium on top of media cost, and nobody checks that it was applied only to the impressions it was billed on. Spend also keeps running while the store can't take orders: merchants report days of normal traffic and zero sales, and paid Google and Facebook traffic landing on a broken checkout. No one connects those dead windows to the invoices, so overbilling and makegoods that could be negotiated go unclaimed.

## Buyer

Finance leads or heads of growth at mid-size online merchants that run paid traffic across several channels and vendors. They sign the media and data invoices but rarely have anyone checking them line by line. They want cash back, and paying a percentage of recovered credits means they take no risk on the budget.

## Proposed service

The client gives read-only exports: invoices, DSP and ad-server delivery reports, order and checkout timestamps. The engine (1) compares billed impressions and clicks with ad-server and analytics counts and flags gaps beyond contract tolerance; (2) checks data-segment fees against the impressions those segments actually targeted; (3) uses order data to rebuild windows where traffic arrived but no orders came through, and marks spend with direct publishers, affiliates and CPC deals in those windows as makegood candidates; (4) writes evidence-backed claim letters for each vendor, which a person sends and follows up. The fee is a percentage of credits or refunds received, so there's no charge if nothing is recovered. Attribution and ROAS dashboards optimize future spend and don't check past invoices, and AP teams can't read delivery logs. It's built from data the client exports, with no platform partnership needed. A pilot is one quarter of invoices for one brand.
