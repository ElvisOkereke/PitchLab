Consider a plumbing company whose website is managed by a small marketing agency.
A potential customer visits the site, fills out a request-for-quote form, and sees a success message. But because of a WordPress plugin update, an SMTP configuration issue, or a CRM integration failure, the lead never reaches the business.

The website is still online. Google Analytics might still record a conversion. An uptime monitor says everything is healthy.
But the business has potentially lost a customer.

The problem isn't website uptime. It's whether the website's lead-generation process is actually working.

There are three distinct levels of monitoring:

Level	    /   What it checks	  /   Limitation
Uptime	 /   Website responds	/  Doesn't detect broken customer journeys
Browser automation	   /   Form can be filled and submitted	/  May not confirm the lead was delivered
Lead-flow verification	  /  Customer action produces the expected downstream result    / 	Requires some knowledge of the destination

The third level is where the potential value lies.

However, the buyer isn't necessarily the plumbing company. It's more likely the agency responsible for maintaining 20–100 client websites, because the agency has repeated exposure to the same problem and a reputation to protect.

The agency would be buying fewer support surprises, evidence that its clients' lead paths work, and a way to demonstrate ongoing maintenance value.
